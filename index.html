The boy from Hammond

It started under a bridge, with a barn pigeon and a dream.

Bernie was born in 1943 in Hammond, Ontario, to one of the original French-Canadian founding families of the area. His father kept pigeons of every kind — Tumblers, Fantails, Tipplers — and the fascination passed straight from father to son. As a boy, Bernie caught wild pigeons in barns and under bridges, hoping to find one wearing a band.

“Getting one with a band, it was like a miracle. I had a special loft to put the ones with bands, and I was trying to breed from them.”— Bernie

When his father went deer hunting in Chalk River — 125 miles away, long before cell phones — he brought pigeons along, tied a little paper message to a leg, and sent the news home on wings. Every night young Bernie checked the loft for a message. His best bird was #1338. He never forgot the number.

In 1966 he married his sweetheart, Suzanne — and celebrated the only way that made sense: he built a loft. The next year, Canada’s centennial, he flew his first race with the Centennial Homing Pigeon Club. He worked 28 years as a messenger in the House of Commons, delivering messages for members of Parliament by day — while at home, he trained the finest messengers of all.

At Bowmanville, against 900 pigeons, his beautiful grizzle cock comes in equal first — $2,000 with a manual clock and a countermark.

2010

The Famous Couple

On Rick Broere’s advice he pairs a $260 Grootjans hen, CU 10/2339, with black cock CU 11/260. That single pairing becomes the foundation of everything that follows.

2015

1st Master Breeder

The Famous Couple’s children start to show what kind of guts they have. Bernie is officially on the map.

2017

Triple Crown winner

His #118 Blue Bar Splash takes the Triple Crown at the Empire Classic. The next year: Master Breeder 1st, 2nd and 3rd.

2019

The dream year

Team “Weekend at Bernie’s” wins the Salem Special 340 — first prize $19,000. Super #303 wins three specials for “Les Deux Suzannes.” Best short, long and overall average. Over $38,750 in a single season.

2023

Still in the cash at 80

Top-three finishes at the Niagara Peninsula and Upper Canada One Loft Races, with three generations of families now flying BB Loft birds.

2026

The chapter closes

After 72 years, Bernie opens the loft doors one last time. The birds fly on — in lofts across Canada, carrying his bloodlines and his lessons with them.

A family affair

Every team on the race sheet was family.

Sue came with Bernie to the one loft race every year — the one trip they always took together. She helped pick the young birds, followed every auction, and was, in his words, his rock. His daughters Josée and Chantal, his grandson Max, Marc and Francine — the whole family watched every race online, kept track of the results while he was away, and minded the loft at home. Win or lose, Bernie never flew alone.
